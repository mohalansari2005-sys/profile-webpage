## What the work actually involved

I joined Majara as a product engineering intern, working hybrid out of
Riyadh. The team is small, so the role spans further than a typical
internship: building backend services in Python, wiring up REST APIs
for the frontend and integrations to consume, and prototyping product
capabilities that started as a requirements conversation rather than
a finished spec.

A lot of the work sat at the intersection of "figure out what's
actually being asked for" and "make it work in production" — reading
a stakeholder's description of a workflow, translating it into a data
model and an API surface, then iterating once real usage showed the
gaps in the first pass. LangChain came in for the pieces that needed
to reason over unstructured input rather than follow a fixed branch of
logic.

The B2B product context mattered: customers are companies, not
individuals, so multi-tenancy, permissions, and predictable behavior
under someone else's business process were constant constraints, not
edge cases to handle later.
