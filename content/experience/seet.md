## What the work actually involved

Before Majara, I spent a few months in business development at SEET
(صيت), a marketing solutions agency in Riyadh. The role was almost the
inverse of engineering work: instead of starting from a technical
requirement, I started from a client conversation and had to figure
out what solution actually fit, then make the case for it.

Day to day that meant running client meetings, putting together sales
pitches and proposal decks, presenting them, and staying in the loop
on the relationship afterward rather than treating a signed deal as
the end of the interaction. Clients rarely describe their problem in a
form that maps directly onto a service line, so a lot of the value was
in translation — turning a vague complaint or goal into a concrete,
sellable scope.

I think about this experience often on the engineering side now: a
badly scoped technical requirement and a badly scoped sales pitch fail
for the same reason, and the fix is the same skill — sit with the
client or stakeholder until you understand what they actually need,
not just what they first said.
